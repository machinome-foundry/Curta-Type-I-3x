How does a Curta add? This short film sets 23 on the setting slides, turns
the crank once, sets 145 and turns it again, and reads 168 on the result
dials. With the case opened in the model, it follows the stepped drum, the
small gears the slides place beside it and the shafts that carry each column
up to its dial. Then it watches the second turn once more beside the Python
that describes the machine in machinome: the slides and the crank as
drivers, the crank's instruction, and the one relation that commits the
result digits at every stroke.

What you see is a simulation of a 3D-printable reproduction at three times
the original size, not a filmed machine. The settings, the strokes and the
digits the dials commit are the model's clocked requests; how the dials roll
in the middle of a stroke is the model's closed-form pose. Forces, friction
and spring dynamics are not simulated. The case is hidden, and parts are
tinted or made translucent, only to show the mechanism.
